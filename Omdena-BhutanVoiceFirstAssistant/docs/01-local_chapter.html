Weekly Tasks & What You Will Learn

Week 1 — Problem Understanding

—Interview or survey 3+ people about a specific public service they struggled with. Document the pain points.
—Map a step-by-step user journey for at least 2 service scenarios (e.g. applying for a permit, accessing health info).
—Write a one-page Problem Definition document. Keep a running log of everything your team did this week — decisions made, methods used, findings.
—You will learn: How to conduct lightweight user research and structure problem statements professionally.

Week 2 — Support Design

—Share user personas and journey maps with Team 2 to ground their conversation flows in real data.
—Validate the two selected use cases. Document which is most realistic and why.

Week 3 — Evaluation Support

—Review prototype flows against your original user journeys. Note gaps and mismatches.
—Produce a one-page "Research vs Prototype" gap analysis and add it to your team's running documentation.

Week 4 — Write Your Section of the Final Report

—Write the "User Needs & Research Findings" section of the Feasibility Report. Submit to Team 4 (report leads) by Day 20.
—Compile all your team's weekly notes into a clean document. This is your team's full contribution record.
—Recommend 1 use case for Phase 2 with a brief justification.
KPI Problem Definition doc + User Journey Map submitted. At least 2 validated use cases documented by Day 10. Team documentation log updated every week.

Weekly Tasks & What You Will Learn

Week 1 — Research Input

—Study Team 1's problem definition. Map what types of questions users will ask the assistant.
—Research examples of chatbot conversation flows for government or civic services.
—Start a team log: one short entry per session covering what you explored and what you decided.
—You will learn: How intent mapping works and how to structure dialogue trees for non-technical users.

Week 2 — Core Design Work

—Design 3 complete conversation flows: opening, service query handling, and fallback/error paths.
—Define user intents and entities (key info the bot needs: name, service type, etc.).
—Present flows at the Week 2 All-Hands for feedback. Revise based on input and document changes.

Week 3 — Iteration

—Work closely with Team 3 to ensure the prototype reflects your conversation design.
—Rewrite any flows that break during prototype testing. Log revision rationale in your team doc.

Week 4 — Write Your Section of the Final Report

—Write the "Conversation Design" section for the Feasibility Report. Submit to Team 4 (report leads) by Day 20.
—Compile all weekly notes into a final team documentation package.
—Recommend the best dialogue approach to carry into Phase 2.
KPI 3 conversation flow drafts reviewed and iterated at least once by end of Week 2. Team documentation log maintained throughout. Zero placeholder flows in the final submission.

Weekly Tasks & What You Will Learn

Week 1 — Tool Selection

—Evaluate 2–3 prototyping tools (e.g. Voiceflow, Botpress, Streamlit + OpenAI API). Compare and decide as a team.
—Set up a shared repo or workspace. Ensure all members can access and contribute.
—Start a team log: document the tool comparison, your final choice, and why.
—You will learn: How to evaluate and select development tools; basic chatbot and voice interface architecture.

Week 2 — First Build

—Implement the first working chatbot draft using Team 2's conversation flows.
—Run 5 internal test sessions. Log every failure — document what breaks and why.
—You will learn: How to integrate LLM APIs; prompt engineering basics; testing methodology for AI interfaces.

Week 3 — Demo-Ready

—Polish the prototype to demo quality. Prepare a 5-minute live walkthrough for Demo Day.
—If time allows: run one voice interaction experiment (even a simple speech-to-text input test counts).
—Collect structured feedback from all 4 teams using a simple form.

Week 4 — Write Your Section of the Final Report

—Write the "Prototype & Testing" section of the Feasibility Report — tools used, how to run the prototype, known limitations, failure logs. Submit to Team 4 by Day 20.
—Submit code + notes to the shared repo. Recommend the tech stack for Phase 2.
—Compile all weekly logs into a final team documentation package.
KPI At least 1 working voice or chat prototype demonstrated live at Week 3 Demo Day (Day 15). Failure logs from at least 5 internal test sessions documented by the team.

Weekly Tasks & What You Will Learn

Week 1 — Scoping the Agent

—Define the categories of service requests the agent should handle (e.g. "passport request", "health info", "NOC").
—Sketch a decision tree on paper first: what does the agent do at each step? What information does it need?
—Start a team log — document scoping decisions as you make them.
—You will learn: Agent architecture basics; intent classification; how LLM-based routing differs from rule-based systems.

Week 2 — Build the Classifier

—Build a request classifier using an LLM API or a simple prompt-based approach. Test with 20 sample requests.
—Define routing logic: given a classified request, which mock "service" does it route to?
—You will learn: Prompt engineering for classification; working with LLM APIs; measuring accuracy.

Week 3 — Integration & Demo

—Attempt to connect your classifier to Team 3's prototype. Even a simple API call integration counts.
—Run an end-to-end test: user input → classification → routing → response. Document accuracy rate and failure cases.
—Show your agent pipeline at Demo Day alongside Team 3's prototype.

Week 4 — Final Report (Team 4 Owns This)

—Write your own "Agent & Workflow" section: architecture, accuracy results, what would need to change at scale.
—Compile the full Feasibility Report by assembling sections received from Teams 1, 2, and 3 by Day 20. Ensure consistency and clarity across all sections.
—Write the Phase 2 Roadmap: recommended use case, proposed tech stack, team structure, timeline estimate.
—Present findings at the Final Showcase (Day 22). This is the primary deliverable of the entire sprint.
KPI At least 1 request classification agent tested end-to-end by Day 17. Accuracy rate documented with real examples. Complete Feasibility Report + Phase 2 Roadmap compiled and presented at Final Showcase (Day 22).

What Is Expected From Every Participant

  • Show up to your team meetings. Attend all scheduled All-Hands and Team Sync calls. If you cannot attend, notify your Team Lead at least 12 hours in advance and send a written update instead.
  • Submit your deliverable every week. Each team has one primary deliverable per week. It does not need to be perfect — it needs to be done. A rough draft submitted on time is worth more than a polished document submitted late.
  • Experiment, don't over-engineer. This sprint is about learning what works, not building a production product. Try things, document what happens, and share what you find — including failures. A failed experiment that is well-documented is a valuable contribution.
  • Communicate early when you're blocked. If you are stuck on something for more than a day, tell your Team Lead. Do not wait until the weekly meeting. Blockers that are raised early can almost always be resolved quickly.
  • Bring your context and perspective. Participants from Bhutan, Nepal, India, Egypt, Nigeria, and elsewhere bring direct knowledge of what public services look like in their country. That context is an asset — share it actively in your team discussions and in the documentation.
  • Document as you go, not at the end. Every team is responsible for their own documentation. Keep short running notes — what you tried, what happened, what you decided. One paragraph per session is enough. Your team's notes become the raw material for your section of the Feasibility Report, so don't leave it to Week 4.
Questions and escalations For anything your Team Lead cannot resolve, contact the project leads directly via the group channel. For documentation questions, reach out to your own Team Lead — each team owns their own docs.
